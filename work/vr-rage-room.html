VR Rage Room

My MSc dissertation: a room full of things to smash, in VR or on a flat screen, built to study how people respond.

my role
Solo: design, programming, testing and every 3D model (made in Blender)
engine
Unity
platform
VR headset and flatscreen (dual mode)
context
MSc Computer Games Technology dissertation, City St George's, University of London
year
2024

The idea

A rage room simulation you can play in VR or on a normal screen. The point wasn't only to make breaking things feel good: it was a research tool for analysing people's emotional responses, so the same experience had to work in both modes and record what players did.

What I built

  • Real-time destruction: procedural destruction plus soft-body physics, so objects deform and break
  • A VR input loop that responds to the player's real-time motion, with physics-based feedback
  • A flatscreen mode running the same simulation, for comparison
  • A UX testing framework, with gameplay-data analysis of each session
  • Every 3D model in the room, made in Blender

The hardest part: getting the physics right

Making things deform and break convincingly was the hardest part, and it all had to run in VR. Dropped frames in a headset are uncomfortable, so the target was a stable 90 FPS. I got there on mid-tier hardware through custom collision mesh design.

What I'd do differently

Split it into different rooms, each built around a different kind of physics, instead of having one room handle everything. Each room could then be tuned on its own.

How it turned out

The project was part of an MSc I finished with a Distinction (71.53%). Holding VR scenes at 90 FPS is now part of my job at Candela.
